# P4 vowel lip sync: offline evidence and the screenshot pass — 2026-10-21

Free and offline: no server, no browser, no paid call. Numbers come from the
shipped code (`src/visemes.ts`, `src/vowel-table.ts`, `src/mouth.ts`) run
under Node on the 24 stored MiniMax streams (22 distinct; speech-2.8-hd,
which is also the configured model): the 4 in `server/fixtures/` and those in
`.data/p5b-check/` (not in git). For the envelope, the MP3s were decoded with
macOS `afconvert`, which keeps 35–60 ms of MP3 priming that a browser decoder
may strip, so the lead check was also run with the audio moved earlier.

What P4 does: the browser gives each Chinese syllable a mouth shape (`a i u e
o`, a narrow `n`, the `m` closure of b/p/m) from the reply's text timings;
the audio still decides how much and when the mouth opens. No wire, server
or dependency change.

## Reading table

`src/vowel-table.ts` (6766 characters: GB2312 L1+L2 plus 呣 欸 齁) against
pinyin-pro 3.29.4 reading each whole reply in context, over the 485 real
replies of the 2026-10-20 live-style runs:

- all 10,517 Han characters are in the table;
- 10,474 (99.59%) get the same key as the in-context reading;
- the rest: 了 le where context says liao ×26 (了解, 不了), 角 ×7 (主角), 着 ×4,
  露 ×2, 行 ×2, 卡 ×1, 还 ×1. Each costs one syllable's shape, never a crash.

`node scripts/build-vowel-table.mjs --check` exits 0 (the table matches the
generator).

## Lead check: MiniMax word times are one token late

R is the voiced share (open ≥ 0.15) of the first readable token's reported
interval; C the same for the interval one token later. The rule: shift when
C − R ≥ 0.2, keep the times when R ≥ 0.5, otherwise no track.

| Audio moved earlier | R max | C min | Smallest C − R | Decisions (24 clips) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 ms (afconvert) | 0.20 | 0.52 | 0.42 | 21 shifted, 3 stale |
| 25 ms | 0.30 | 0.48 | 0.28 | 21 shifted, 3 stale |
| 50 ms | 0.40 | 0.45 | 0.15 | 19 shifted, 2 no track, 3 stale |

- No clip took the as-reported branch. Moving the audio earlier only made a
  clip fall back to today's mouth, never take the other branch.
- The 2 at 50 ms are the same clip twice (the laugh capture, `fixture
  joined-laugh` = `act-laugh`): its first readable token is `(laughs)`,
  whose interval holds the laugh itself (R 0.30, C 0.45). **Play capture of
  joined-laugh in the browser decides whether this happens on the real
  decoder** (screenshot pass, item 7).

## Stale tail

A clip whose subtitles stop more than 1.0 s before the audio ends gets no
track (the final subtitle update came without word times, so the server kept
an early snapshot whose tail words are 43 ms placeholders).

- Decoded duration − last word end: healthy 0.22–0.50 s on 21 clips; stale
  1.83 s (fixture cumulative), 2.62 s (`name-none`), 3.23 s
  (`strawberry-1-joined`).
- `node scripts/viseme-check.mjs --dry-run`, which uses the stream's
  `audio_length` instead: healthy 0.18–0.43 s, stale 1.77 / 2.57 / 3.16 s.
  Same three clips, a wide margin either way.

## Coverage and opening

On the 21 tracked clips (afconvert decoding):

- 91.3% of voiced frames carry a shape. Chinese-only clips: 99–100%; the
  laugh clip 79% (the laugh is blank). Clips with a name or Latin text:
  53–72%, because `Hiyori` (×5 under the
  dictionary rule), `日和`, `he2` and Latin chunks are blank units that keep
  the audio form.
- With Hiyori's starting values (`active.json`), the time-weighted mouth
  opening is 0.887 of today's (the design expected about 0.86): i, u and n
  are narrower, a is wider.

## Client contract, as built

- `src/speech.ts`: `frames = withVowels(mouthFrames(data, sampleRate),
  alignment, duration)` in `consumeSegments`, after the pause silence and the
  shifted timings, so a pause moves the track by exactly 16 frames. Ready
  clips, the completed replay, seeks and cancel carry the shapes on the
  frames. Open and form are copied unchanged, so the beats are identical.
- `onMouth(open, form, beat, beatAhead, vowel)`: five arguments on every
  audio-playback frame (`vowel` null where the frame has none); stops stay
  `(0, 0, null)` and browser speech keeps its three arguments.
- `src/App.vue`, `src/stage/main.ts`: `latestMouth.vowel` (null after a stop)
  is passed to `setMouth` and re-applied when the avatar mounts.
- Tests: `src/speech.test.mjs` (five arguments and the exact vowel per frame;
  Kokoro, cloud `words: []` and empty alignment give null with unchanged open
  and form; a pause shows no vowel before 0.4 s; each clip keeps its own
  vowels through the completed replay and a backward seek; beats identical
  with and without a track), `scripts/viseme-check.test.mjs` (happy-short
  passes the client decoder's word checks, cumulative is stale), plus the
  table, viseme, profile and engine tests.

## Review fixes (2026-10-22)

An adversarial review (three lenses, two skeptics per finding) left five
findings, all fixed:

- **Lead check order.** A clip is now shifted only when its first word's
  reported interval is mostly silent (R < 0.5); an on-time first word with a
  soft onset (我, R 0.67) keeps its timings instead of being moved one
  syllable late. This matters if a model (turbo is the `.env.example`
  default) reports on time. Re-run on the 24 hd captures: the same decisions
  as before (21 lead 1 and 3 stale at 0 and 25 ms; at 50 ms two laugh clips
  become ambiguous, as before).
- **Readings.** 呗 is bei (pinyin-pro: bai), 嗨 hai (hei), 喔 o (wo); the table
  was regenerated.
- **Debug page.** A vowels tour cut off by a faces tour now releases its
  vowel hold; Play capture plays an ElevenLabs-style response (characters
  only) and a timing-less one. Both checked in headless Chrome.
- **Docs.** `inspect().mouth.lead` stays null; the per-clip decision is on
  the clip-tracks line.

## Not verified

- Mouth motion during real playback (test line, flicker, where the b/p/m
  closure lands at 30 fps): needs a real browser window or OBS.
- Eleven character timings: no real capture exists, so they are used as
  reported, with no lead check.
- Listener-rated naturalness: none of the above says the mouth looks right,
  only that the shapes land on the voiced frames of the right syllables.
